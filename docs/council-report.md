# Council Report

Question: Is Khanmigo very similar to the Young Lady's Illustrated Primer in Neal Stephenson's The Diamond Age, and how might Stephenson's vision deliver where Khanmigo failed?

## Animal Perspectives

### Bear: Vision & Direction
Same pitch, different product. Khanmigo is a help button for maths practice, judged on test percentiles in a remedial block. The Primer sets out to raise a whole person over about a decade, and was commissioned to make a child subversive. The Primer leads; Khanmigo waits to be asked.

### Cat: Risk & Scope
The Primer is fiction and still only works for one girl in three. The scaled, human-free version produces the Mouse Army: capable, uniform, loyal to someone else. Nell's outcome depends on Miranda, a human who voices her book for years. Stephenson's own advice: "Kids need to get answers from humans who love them." A bonded, always-on AI for children is also a manipulation risk, and there is no evidence yet that engaged AI tutoring adds learning.

### Owl: Process & Structure
Four structural differences. Initiative: Khanmigo was one click away and used on a third of practice days; the Primer initiates. Motivation: Khanmigo withheld answers and lost students; the Primer makes the problem the plot. Continuity: years with one child versus a school term. Failure: the Primer lets Nell fail and persist inside the story.

### Puppy: Opportunities & Momentum
Voice, memory and adaptive story are now cheap. Khan's redesign is moving toward the Primer by building the tutor into practice. A multi-year narrative where the maths is the plot has not been tried and is testable.

### Rabbit: Resources & Stakeholders
The missing resource is the ractor: paid human attention behind the voice. Nell also had Harv, Constable Moore and Miss Matheson's academy. The Tennessee students wanted answers; Nell wanted escape. The Primer met a need she felt.

### Wolf: Cohesion & Balance
The comparison overweights the device and underweights the surrounding people. Peers are neglected, and the book treats the Primer's solitude as a cost. Khanmigo kept productive struggle and lost the students; the Primer buys struggle with story.

## Consensus

Khanmigo and the Primer are not very similar. Khanmigo is a patient help desk; the Primer is a relationship plus a world. Khanmigo failed on engagement, which is where the Primer's design differs most: it initiates, wraps lessons in a personal story, persists for years, and has a caring human behind it.

The tension: what makes the Primer work is what makes it expensive and risky, and scaling it by removing the human gives the Mouse Army. Stephenson has since called the book's optimism a product of 1990s techno-utopianism and argues AI education that increases dependence is not education.

Overall: the Primer's design would probably fix the engagement problem, but the technology is necessary, not sufficient. The human is the active ingredient.

## Recommended Next Steps

1. Frame the goal as AI plus a caring human, not AI instead of one.
2. Watch for trials of the redesigned Khanmigo, which test initiative on its own.
3. Treat narrative as the untested lever and the place a new experiment would add something.
4. Keep engagement and learning separate when reading any claim.
