# How it would be used

A typical week with a seven-year-old and a story box on the shelf.

## Once, at setup

A parent names the people in the circle (Mum, Grandad, big cousin), picks a story world, and records a few short voice clips. The box runs a playful ten-minute check to find her level.

## Most evenings, about 15 minutes

1. She presses the button. The narrator picks up yesterday's chapter.
2. Her character reaches a bridge. The troll will only lower it if 12 coins are shared fairly between 3 travellers.
3. She answers out loud. She's wrong, so the troll laughs and the bridge stays up. A character drops a hint.
4. Second try, she gets it. The bridge lowers and the chapter ends on a hook.
5. The box goes quiet until tomorrow.

## Straight after, on the parent's phone

> She cracked sharing 12 between 3 on her second go. At dinner, ask how she'd share 12 biscuits between 4.

## At dinner

The parent asks. That two-minute chat is the most important part of the system. If they like, they record a 20-second clip that turns up tomorrow as a message from the Queen.

## Weekend

A quest off the box: halve a pikelet recipe with Grandad, then tell the story what happened.

## Every few weeks

A cold check on paper with no story and no hints. The parent sees whether the skills hold without the box.

## End of term

The story ends properly. The next arc starts with fewer hints and more of her own choices.

## In short

Fifteen minutes of story, two minutes of a parent's attention aimed at the right thing, and one real-world task a week.
