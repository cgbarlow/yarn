# Background

## What happened with Khanmigo

Khanmigo is Khan Academy's AI tutor, released in 2023. It coaches with questions and hints and does not hand over answers.

The main independent study is "One Click Away" by Philip Oreopoulos and Nina Low (NBER working paper, August 2026). It was a two-year cluster randomised trial in 18 Tennessee middle schools, with students using Khan Academy plus Khanmigo during daily remedial maths sessions.

Findings:

- Maths achievement rose by about 0.06 to 0.08 standard deviations over a school year.
- Those gains look like what Khan Academy practice delivers without AI.
- 96 percent of students tried Khanmigo at least once. The median student messaged it on only a third of practice days, and in 17 percent of sessions where they made a mistake.
- Most messages were bare answers or clicks on suggested prompts. When it refused to give answers, students largely stopped using it.
- The authors' conclusion: engagement is the binding constraint. Access is not enough.

Khan Academy's response: Sal Khan said the findings matched what they saw internally. The tested version dated from 2023. Khanmigo has since been built into practice rather than sitting in a separate tab.

Still open: whether students who do use an AI tutor regularly learn more.

## How the Primer differs

The Primer in *The Diamond Age* is often cited as the model for AI tutors. It is not much like Khanmigo.

| | Khanmigo (as tested) | The Primer |
|---|---|---|
| Ambition | Help with maths practice | Raise a whole person over a decade |
| Goal | Test scores | A child who can think against her own culture |
| Initiative | Waits to be asked | Starts the conversation and stays |
| Motivation | Withholds answers, asks questions | The lesson is the plot of a story about the child |
| Time span | A school term | Years with one child |
| Failure | Students walked away | The child fails inside the story and keeps going |
| Human | None | Miranda, a human actor who voices the book for years |

## What the book itself warns

- The Primer goes one for three. Two of the three girls drift away. One becomes extraordinary.
- Nell's result depends on Miranda, the human behind the voice, and on other people around her: Harv, Constable Moore, Miss Matheson's academy.
- The mass version, hundreds of thousands of copies with synthetic voices, produces the Mouse Army: capable, uniform, loyal to someone else.

## Stephenson's later thinking

- Asked what a real Primer must get right, he answered: "Kids need to get answers from humans who love them."
- In "Emerson, AI, and The Force" (July 2025) he called the book's optimism a product of 1990s techno-utopianism.
- He argues AI education that increases dependence is not education at all, and that self-reliance comes from attempting hard things and sometimes failing, with good mentors and peers.
- His worry about current tools is that they treat failure as a malfunction.

## The conclusion Yarn starts from

Khanmigo is a patient help desk. The Primer is a relationship plus a world. The Primer's design would probably fix the engagement problem, but the technology is necessary, not sufficient. The human is the active ingredient.
