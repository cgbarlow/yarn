# Prior art

From a quick scan in November 2026. Pieces of Yarn exist. The whole thing does not appear to. Small projects are easy to miss, so a proper landscape review should come before any build.

## Closest in spirit

**The Primer (open source, primer-ai.org).** A voice-first, Socratic companion named after the book. Runs entirely on-device with no streaks or notifications. A dedicated child device is on its roadmap. It teaches by asking questions, which is the Khanmigo approach, with no story and little role for parents.

## Story plus learning

**StoryMate (Northeastern University).** An AI that reads with a child and pauses to ask questions. Parents said it helped them ask better questions themselves. Being tested in two California school districts. The nearest thing to the human channel working.

**Tinker Tales (research prototype).** A board game for ages 4 to 6 with NFC tokens and an AI narrator. Parents enter a lesson and it gets woven into the story.

**App Store story apps.** Dozens generate personalised stories. None found gate the plot on a skill or run for years.

## Screen-free boxes

Yoto and Toniebox prove the form with no AI. Newer AI story players generate tales on demand but carry no curriculum.

## The parent channel

Stanford's text-message tips for parents of preschoolers are the best evidence for the ractor channel. Trials reportedly found small but real literacy gains from a few texts a week. No story, no AI, just well-aimed prompts. (Not re-verified in this scan. Check the papers before relying on it.)

## The gap

Nobody has joined the four parts:

1. A multi-year story where skills move the plot
2. Code-verified challenges
3. A daily prompt to a parent
4. A planned ending

Each part has been shown to work alone. The new work is in combining them.
